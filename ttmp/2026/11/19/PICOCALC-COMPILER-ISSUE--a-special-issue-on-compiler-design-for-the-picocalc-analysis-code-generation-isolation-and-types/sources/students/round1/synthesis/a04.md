## 1. Errors and inconsistencies in the article

- **11,760 counted both ways (1/3).** The table offers "11,760 function visits" as waste; then "Of the 11,760 function visits for Agenda, 9,019 were skipped because the function's inputs had not changed." Only 2,741 (23%) analyzed a body. That is the waste figure, and the base of "62 percent of the remaining visits", which 2/3 could not place.
- **"A summary must be recomputed only when the function's own text changes." (1/3)** s1 objected that a function depends on what it calls. Checked: a summary is "how its outputs depend on its inputs", and `nextIndex` returns `clamp(i + 1, n)`, so editing `clamp` changes it. True of the jump functions shown, not in general. The key to question 2 requires this sentence.
- **Demand row unhedged (1/3).** Table: "Same answers for the questions asked". Text: "up to a bound on search effort beyond which it gives up and keeps the check".
- **"The measurements of Article 3 identify five distinct ways" (*grader*).** Article 3 has 11,760, 95, 11.7 million and 170 s, but not 78.3%, 39.6%, 9,019 or the cache experiment. Two "Evidence" cells are not measurements ("Each build starts from nothing").
- **"round" shifts meaning (*grader*).** Article 3 has six specialization rounds and 95 "Fixpoint visits of the whole program"; here "95 whole-program rounds".
- **"No; precision can improve" (*grader*)** answers "Does the answer change?" both ways.
- **Missing references (1/3; checked).** MicroPython, AssemblyScript, OCaml, `isolatedDeclarations`.
