## 1. Errors and inconsistencies in the article

- **58 percent against "about 1.2 s" (3/3).** "it removes the frontend, which Experiment 3 found to be about 58 percent of the native compile path": 42% of 3.14 s is 1.3 s; the table says "about 1.2 s". Confirmed: 1.2 s is 3.14 − 1.99 on the device, 58% a workstation share (Article 2, section 2.4).
- **"about 0.01 s to load, plus hashing" against "reuse of the stored module took about 1,450 ms at boot" (2/3: s1, s2).** The article calls the second "the one to expect for the typed compiler". Confirmed; nothing says what the 1.45 s contains.
- **Five inputs, three stored fields (2/3: s1, s2).** "The record holds the hash of the source, the hash of the compiled module, and the identity of the firmware build". Confirmed: nothing says the firmware identity covers compiler, reactor and function table.
- **Two slots (2/3: s2, s3).** "in one of two slots" and "leaves the old record pointing at the old, intact module". Confirmed: the article never says the new module goes to the other slot; written in place, the claim is false. Question 2 rests on it.
- **4.76 against 240/88 = 2.7 (3/3).** "4.76 bytes of RV32 per byte of WebAssembly function body" and "88 kB for Editor against 240 kB" use different denominators. Confirmed consequence (s1): "between 1.4 and 1.8 times the size of the WebAssembly" is not in Article 5; it is 4.76/3.32 and 4.76/2.66, per body byte.
- **"a peak of 1.5 MB … and 2.5 MB" against "768 KiB for Editor" (1/3: s3)**: whose memory does the compiler use?
- **"can almost compensate" beside "almost twice the time" (1/3: s1)**: opposite impressions in one sentence.
- **"one instance of this idea already, applied at boot instead of at build" (1/3: s2)**: a cache, in a section about work before boot.
- **Three names (1/3: s3)**: "table of functions the reactor offers", "import table", "the reactor's function table".
- **Units (2/3: s2, s3)**: "8 MiB and holds about 4.4 MB".
- Grader only: "under 20 ms" per function uses the WebAssembly build; native is 3.14 s / 120 = 26 ms.
